## ⏱️ Timeline & Choreography Breakdown

| Time | Scene | On-Screen Action | Voiceover Narration |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **0:00 - 0:25** | **The Problem Hook** | Live app loaded at Connaught Place. Highlight 43.5°C temperature, 59.8°C heat index red alert. | *"During peak Indian summers, temperatures routinely cross 44 degrees Celsius. For delivery gig workers, laborers, and pedestrians walking to metro stations, standard navigation apps like Google Maps optimize purely for vehicular speed, routing vulnerable walkers through scorching unshaded asphalt where perceived heat index exceeds 58 degrees. Google Maps tells you the fastest way there; HeatRoute tells you the safer way there when heat matters."* |
| **0:25 - 0:55** | **The Core Trade-Off** | Click 'Barakhamba Road Direct' (High Exposure), then click 'Kasturba Gandhi Marg' (Balanced). | *"Here on our central Delhi corridor from Rajiv Chowk to Mandi House, Barakhamba Road is the fastest direct path, but exposes walkers to unshaded blacktop asphalt and high thermal radiation. In contrast, HeatRoute evaluates Kasturba Gandhi Marg. By leveraging sixty-five percent heritage tree canopy cover, it slashes thermal strain by 22.4 percent for a minor detour of just two and a half minutes."* |
| **0:55 - 1:20** | **Safety-First Filter** | Show disqualified route card or explain why dangerous shortcuts are rejected. | *"Crucially, HeatRoute enforces a hard safety filter. A vehicular expressway flyover shortcut might be mathematically faster, but is instantly disqualified because it lacks sidewalks and pedestrian infrastructure. Safety first, heat second, speed third."* |
| **1:20 - 1:50** | **Diurnal Solar Time-Travel** | Drag bottom time slider from 09:00 AM to 01:30 PM (Solar Apex) to 05:00 PM. | *"Microclimates aren't static. Watch our diurnal solar engine in action: as we drag the time slider across the day, the solar azimuth and altitude angles recalculate dynamically in real time. Building canyon shadows shift, turning previously exposed street segments into protected cool corridors by late afternoon."* |
| **1:50 - 2:15** | **Track 02 Hydration Recovery Layer** | Toggle 'Water & Rest Stops' flyout on the right. Highlight Bangla Sahib & DJB water points. | *"Heat navigation requires active physical recovery. For Track 02, HeatRoute integrates verified municipal and community hydration shelters: free chilled RO water at Gurudwara Bangla Sahib, Delhi Jal Board public water posts, and metro station water ATMs with one-click pitstop routing."* |
| **2:15 - 2:40** | **Dual-Hazard Monsoon Mode** | Click 'Monsoon Flood' toggle in the topbar. | *"When the season changes from heatwave to torrential monsoon, HeatRoute seamlessly switches to urban flood mode. It pinpoints severe waterlogging in railway underpasses and sunken roads, rerouting pedestrians through covered colonnades and elevated heritage walkways."* |
| **2:40 - 3:00** | **AWS Architecture & Closing** | Click 'AWS Telemetry' modal in the top right. | *"Under the hood, HeatRoute is powered entirely by AWS: Amazon Location Service streams dark vector navigation tiles, AWS Lambda with Python executes biometeorological exposure scoring in milliseconds, Amazon DynamoDB manages verified hydration shelters, and the frontend is deployed globally on AWS Amplify. Built for the heat that dries the tap and the monsoon that floods the street. Thank you."* |
